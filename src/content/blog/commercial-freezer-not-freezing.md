![Three ways a commercial freezer fails to hold zero degrees: cold but not cold enough, running nonstop and still warm, or not running at all, each with its likely causes](/blog/freezer-not-freezing.svg)

A freezer that will not freeze is a different problem from a cooler that runs warm, and it gets expensive faster. Frozen product has further to fall and less room to recover. The useful first step is not guessing at parts, it is working out which of three failures you actually have, because each one sends a tech down a different path.

## First, get the real number

Commercial freezers generally hold around 0&#176;F, and the test that matters is whether the food is frozen solid, not what the dial says. Put a probe thermometer between two packages near the middle of the box and give it a few minutes. Note the reading and the time.

Then stand still and listen. Is the unit running? That one question splits the problem three ways.

## Case 1: cold, but not cold enough

The box is holding somewhere in the teens or twenties, the compressor cycles normally, and nothing sounds wrong. The system is working, it just cannot get all the way down. Three things cause most of this:

- **A packed condenser coil.** The coil has to shed heat, and it cannot do that through a blanket of dust and grease. This is the single most common cause, and it is the one you can check yourself.
- **A door gasket that no longer seals.** Close the door on a dollar bill and pull. If it slides out freely, warm air is bleeding in all day.
- **Refrigerant running low.** A slow leak shows up as a box that used to hold zero and now settles at fifteen. This one needs a tech, because the refrigerant went somewhere.

## Case 2: running nonstop, still warm

The unit never seems to shut off, and the box is still warm. Running constantly is not the unit trying harder. It means it is losing.

Open the door and look at the evaporator coil inside. If it is buried in ice, air cannot move through it, and a freezer with no airflow warms up no matter how hard the compressor works. That usually traces to a defrost problem: a failed heater, a bad termination sensor, or a defrost cycle that has stopped running at all.

If the coil is clear, look at what is in front of it. Boxes stacked against the evaporator or the air return will strangle circulation just as effectively as ice. A freezer loaded to the ceiling after a delivery can warm up for no reason other than that the air has nowhere to go.

Do not chip at an iced coil with anything metal. It is easy to puncture the coil and turn a defrost repair into a refrigerant repair.

## Case 3: not running at all

Silence is its own diagnosis. Check the breaker and confirm the unit has power, and check that the thermostat has not been knocked to an off or wrong setting, which happens more often than anyone admits.

If it has power and still will not start, stop there. A compressor that hums, clicks rapidly, and quits is trying to start and failing, and repeatedly resetting the breaker to make it try again can turn a start component into a dead compressor. That is a call, not a reset.

## Protect the product while you work

Whatever the cause, keep the door shut. A full freezer holds its temperature far longer than an empty one, so resist the urge to keep opening it to check. Move the most valuable product to another freezer if you have the space, and write down the time and temperature as you go. If the box has been above freezing long enough that you are unsure about the food, that call belongs to you and your local health authority, not to a repair tech.

## What keeps this from happening again

Nearly every version of Case 1 and Case 2 above starts as something visible weeks earlier: a coil going grey with dust, a gasket that has gone hard, a defrost cycle that quietly stopped terminating.
